`utrecht-small.json` is a deterministic local import of the already pinned Utrecht PBF (source SHA-256 `8522f893d9c9c201d19d8fc0e39a038ad25fb21f6832370d4d257b4b495b9fa7`). Artifact SHA-256: `56ad8d36d84e234984d335bd24a5abba50f867d96b1a0281ef54ffc792666cbf`.

The 100 m buffered rectangular study extent retains 452 complete selected ways, 1,246 referenced nodes, nine restriction relations touching those ways, and 74 explicit boundary-cut segments. Source attribution is © OpenStreetMap contributors under ODbL 1.0. The original OSM extraction date is unknown in the upstream Valhalla test data and remains `null`; retrieval date is not substituted. This Utrecht sample is not Philippine road validation or a production regional import. Regional parsing/geometry tools named in the architecture still require exact-version approval.

## Talayan, Quezon City sample (Task 3.5)

`talayan-20261013.osm.pbf` is a 44,094-byte deterministic derivative of the [Metro Manila OSM extract](https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts/asia/philippines/metro_manila.osm.pbf). The public regional file's published MD5 `7578119c5adbf437b71d74cc8ba02b97` matched the downloaded 61,170,191 bytes; its measured SHA-256 is `b3ce4246589f163b46673f77f1c0fa29f52f982529fe1e151fa126ec89a8e7a6`. The adjacent replication state gave `2026-10-13T00:59:48Z`. The original regional file remains ignored in `.runtime/osm/`. The derived PBF SHA-256 is `fbea4907750e0a10be7be6a7b9188ec91d5c74c9790ab600ea02265434f73ae6`; `talayan-small.json` SHA-256 is `f9f552f43feaa2de02ea3f0eda97ca85cbd06a3c0ab5e6289483731e8c18ecbb`. Both files are ODbL 1.0, © OpenStreetMap contributors.

The initial *rectangular routing sample* is latitude 14.629–14.638, longitude 121.005–121.018 with a 250 m buffer, centered on the plan-listed flood-priority barangay Talayan. It is not an official Talayan administrative boundary or a population allocation. The pinned import has 608 complete ways, 2,288 referenced nodes, eight touching restriction relations, and 101 explicit boundary-cut segments. `services/osm-import/build_small_pbf.py` repacks only those selected objects from the verified regional import artifact; rerunning it yields the same derived PBF hash. Importing that PBF twice yields identical `talayan-small.json` bytes. Task 3.7 must review the actual modeled boundary, population/resource membership, connectors and exclusions before any simulation. Regional production execution and unapproved geo packages are outside this small-sample proof.

## Graph review (Task 3.6)

`utrecht-compiled-review.json` and `talayan-compiled-review.json` pin the exact-edge car graph counts, explicit 30 km/h fallback, boundary edges, and review issues. Talayan compiles to 1,848 routable nodes, 3,272 directed edges, 12 conservative turn rules and 607 boundary-cut edges. The 318 unknown-speed ways use the declared fallback; 36 shared nodes span different OSM layer tags and require connector review in 3.7. One unsupported restriction form blocks its entry exits. Bridge assets group all compiled segments/directions of each bridge way; named closures remove those exact edges.

The older Task 2.4 Utrecht probe reported a 122-second reverse bridge-closure detour. Its route used OSM way `7011033`, tagged `access=no; psv=yes`; it is not usable by the car profile. The reviewed car route is 135 seconds and 1,398,920 mm. The forward-open, forward-closed and reverse-open car routes remain identical to the earlier probe. The historical benchmark is retained as an audit trail; the corrected four-case car reference is in `utrecht-compiled-review.json`.

## Guiguinto source and local scenario scopes (Task 3.7)

The [Central Luzon OSM extract](https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts/asia/philippines/central_luzon.osm.pbf) was 101,794,244 bytes, with published MD5 `723ee55f9f83bf389cda2136b91267bb` matching the local download, SHA-256 `d3cbf4f9c0cc26c6d10cf074aa797d9de8b04972cfc9c6b3434cd490807bf1e7`, and replication timestamp `2026-10-13T00:59:48Z`. The full file stays ignored in `.runtime/osm/`. Its tracked Guiguinto derivative is 21,968 bytes with SHA-256 `4b4ceb0440f06b92ae4edcaf33ca970e8a685c13cc61281d929ecda5d086ae75`, preserving 295 complete ways, 1,436 nodes and 62 cuts in the 250 m buffered Poblacion-centered rectangle; no turn restriction relation touches this specific small road subset. Both files are ODbL 1.0, © OpenStreetMap contributors.

`pilot-areas.json` records two local, personally reviewable *model cells*: a Talayan-centered QC rectangle (latitude 14.629–14.638, longitude 121.005–121.018) and a Poblacion-centered Guiguinto rectangle (latitude 14.823–14.832, longitude 120.874–120.884). These are modeling boundaries, not administrative barangay polygons. Each includes exactly one hypothetical 100-person cohort and excludes everyone else; neither city-wide nor municipal totals are allocated. The QC pickup and illustrative shelter anchors connect to exact OSM nodes 219 m and 11 m away; their uncut car route is 211 s/2,053,257 mm. The Guiguinto representative pickup and approximate GMACC anchors connect at 24 m and 25 m; their uncut car route is 64 s/569,296 mm. The [Talayan representative coordinate](https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/ncr/quezon-city/talayan.html) and [approximate GMACC coordinate](https://philippines.worldplaces.me/places-in-guiguinto/29325383-guiguinto-municipal-athletic-and-cultural-center.html) support approximate positioning only. A Poblacion pickup coordinate and QC shelter anchor are explicit exploratory choices. OSM road proximity does not prove walking access, an open facility, capacity, neighboring-resource permission, or field conditions. Those remain visible assumptions in the pilot record and review UI.

The local geography API derives a scope hash from the study-area review, pinned import bytes and exact-edge graph. Confirming a case's scope updates only that owner/case's evidence draft, clears assumptions tied to its former pending scope, and requires a new seal and acknowledgment. The map is a schematic overlay with a matching table; tile failure leaves both available. Geoapify suggestions use a backend-only key when configured and are never automatically accepted; the no-key manual path was used for these two records.
